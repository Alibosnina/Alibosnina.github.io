Profile

Who I am

I’m a computer science student at FH Aachen, and most of what I know I learned by building systems that had to actually work. A freight platform moving goods between Europe and Libya. An IFRS-compatible construction ledger. A national electronic health record built on FHIR. Each one started as an empty repository and ended as something deployed, documented and running.

The pattern across all of them is the same stack and the same discipline: a NestJS API over PostgreSQL, a Next.js or React front end, Docker for everything underneath, and a permission model thought through before the first screen gets built. Several are bilingual English/Arabic with right-to-left layouts throughout. Alongside that I worked as a Werkstudent at Snacc-it GmbH, tutored first-year programming at FH Aachen, and ran IT operations for a small company for two years.

Logistics 2025 – 2026

Fox Line Logistics

Freight platform · Europe ↔ Libya

An end-to-end shipping platform covering the whole journey: customer shipment intake, warehouse operations, consolidation and routing, customs paperwork, delivery and tracking — with a full double-entry accounting back office behind it. The ledger is genuine bookkeeping: a chart of accounts, journal entries whose lines must balance, monthly periods that lock once closed, and cost centres attributing activity to a warehouse, shipment, client or department. Balances are never adjusted directly; you post an entry.

Built as an npm-workspaces monorepo — one NestJS API and two Next.js front ends (a staff dashboard for operations, CRM and finance, and a customer portal with public tracking) — sharing a UI package and a typed contract of enums, DTOs and permission codes. Access is governed by an attribute-based permission model, and the whole system is documented down to the database and deployment level.

Accounting 2026

Mumtaz Ledger

Enterprise construction accounting

An audit-proof, IFRS-compatible accounting system for construction, designed to scale across multiple countries. The data model is deliberately strict: a Company → Legal Entity → Location hierarchy, hierarchical general-ledger accounts carrying English, Arabic and Turkish labels, cost centres, and projects bound to legal entities.

NestJS and Next.js in a monorepo over PostgreSQL with Prisma, identity handled by a self-hosted Keycloak, and a Material UI dashboard. Deployed to a VPS behind Caddy with automatic Let’s Encrypt certificates, HTTP/3 and enforced HTTPS.
